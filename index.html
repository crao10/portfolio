The Challenge

The Problem Space

The world's most ambitious technological breakthroughs—quantum computing, carbon capture, satellite systems, cancer therapeutics, AI—emerge from brilliant technical founders: PhDs, professors, and researchers who've spent decades at the frontier of scientific discovery.

Yet as economists from Schumpeter to Teece have documented, scientific excellence and commercial viability require fundamentally different capabilities. The "valley of death" between laboratory breakthrough and market impact isn't a failure of vision—it's the challenge of translating technical novelty into investable ventures, articulating value propositions that resonate beyond academia, and building the partnerships that transform research into scaled solutions.

Space

Autonomous Medical Assistant for Extreme Environments

The Challenge: An ER physician-founder built a resuscitation app with the goal of launching it in space and creating the first autonomous medical assistant for space applications. He had already won a Canadian Space Agency contract, but was facing investor skepticism about the path from hospital app to ISS-ready platform.

My Work:

  • • Embedded as strategic thought partner with the founding team
  • • Recruited space medicine experts to validate the technical roadmap
  • • Conducted market analysis across adjacent extreme environment sectors (military, maritime, remote operations)
  • • Shaped a phased investment narrative with clear value inflection points: resuscitation MVP → comprehensive autonomous assistant
  • • Translated technical requirements into an investable business roadmap

Climate & Life Sciences

Cultivated Protein with Functional Fat Integration

The Challenge: A biotech startup developed a breakthrough method for incorporating functional adipose tissue into cultivated protein—solving one of the cellular agriculture industry's hardest technical problems: replicating the marbling and mouthfeel of conventional meat. While the science was validated in small-scale bioreactors, they faced the classic commercialization valley of death: unclear regulatory pathway, capital-intensive scale-up requirements, and a market skeptical of "lab-grown meat" economics.

My Work:

  • • Technical validation with domain experts: Leveraged my biochemistry background to interview the founders on their differentiation protocols and lipid integration mechanisms. Recruited cellular agriculture researchers and food scientists to assess the technology's scalability and regulatory feasibility.
  • • Market discovery & commercialization strategy: Conducted stakeholder interviews with food manufacturers, cultivated meat industry veterans, and regulatory consultants to map the unique challenges—multi-year FDA approval timelines, capital requirements for GMP manufacturing facilities, and consumer acceptance hurdles.
  • • GTM sequencing & business model: Rather than pursuing direct-to-consumer (which requires massive capital for both production scale and consumer education), shaped a B2B2C strategy: target premium restaurant partnerships first to build proof-of-concept and brand credibility before retail launch.
  • • Financial modeling: Built market sizing across premium meat segments, modeled unit economics at different production scales (1,000L vs 10,000L bioreactors), and developed pro forma P&Ls showing the path from pilot facility to commercial profitability.
  • • Translated technical innovation to commercial narrative: Articulated the value proposition beyond "lab-grown"—positioned as sustainable protein with superior consistency, traceability, and customizable nutritional profiles that conventional agriculture cannot achieve.
Seed funding secured | Regulatory pathway validated | Strategic partnership discussions with food conglomerate

Recruited domain experts in cryptography and distributed systems to validate technical claims. Developed evaluation frameworks to assess both technical novelty and commercial viability across zero-knowledge proofs, post-quantum cryptography, and AI security applications.

Interviewed founders from around the world building breakthrough carbon capture, renewable energy, and sustainable materials technologies. Assessed market readiness, GTM strategies, and investment potential for technologies competing for global recognition.
  • • Systematic methodology transfer: Created repeatable frameworks for due diligence that non-technical team members could apply—translating complex technical concepts (consensus mechanisms, lattice-based cryptography, direct air capture technology) into business evaluation criteria.
